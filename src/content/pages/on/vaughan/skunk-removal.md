Skunks are lazy diggers, and I mean that as a compliment. They'd much rather move into a hole somebody else made than start one from scratch. Give a skunk a dark, dry space with a bit of soft dirt at the entrance and it'll take care of the rest overnight.

That's a big reason skunk removal in Vaughan is such a regular job for us. So many streets here are newer subdivisions, built on fill that's still settling, with decks, front steps and sheds that went in fast. Lots of them back right onto ravines and the Don valley lands, which is skunk country. A Thornhill backyard or a new street in Patterson can have a skunk under the deck within a couple of seasons of the sod going down.

So let's go through it the way I would if we were walking your yard together. How to tell it's a skunk, why spring is a different job from fall, how we get it out without anyone getting sprayed, and how to stop the next one from moving into the same spot.

## Skunk, raccoon or groundhog?

Before anything else, make sure of what you've got. All three like the space under a deck.

**It's probably a skunk if you see:**

- Small, cone-shaped holes poked into the lawn overnight, especially after rain. That's a skunk nosing for grubs.
- An oval hole roughly 10 to 20 cm wide at the edge of a deck, step or shed, with smooth, packed edges and some loose soil pushed out.
- A worn little path through the grass or mulch leading to that hole.
- Five-toed tracks with long front claw marks in mud or snow.
- A musky smell near the opening, even when nobody's been sprayed.

**It's probably a raccoon** if the sod is peeled back in strips or the garbage is tipped. **It's probably a groundhog** if there's a big mound of dirt at the hole and you see it in broad daylight. Skunks are night animals.

Don't crawl under the deck to check. A flashlight from a safe distance at dusk, or a cheap trail camera aimed at the hole overnight, will tell you plenty. You can also just send us a photo.

## New subdivisions and settling soil

Vaughan has its own skunk recipe. When a subdivision goes in, the ground around the foundation is backfilled, and that fill keeps settling for years. Add a deck built straight on top of it, a shed on a few patio slabs, or a set of front steps sitting on a pad, and you've got hollow spots and soft soil right where a skunk wants them.

The other half is the neighbours. Houses backing onto the ravines and stormwater ponds, from the Don valley lands around Thornhill out to the Humber side of the city, have wild space right behind the fence. And the yards themselves are full of food: grubs in new sod, pet food, bird seed, compost and fallen fruit.

The weak spots I see most around here:

- Decks with no skirting, or lattice skirting the skunk has already pushed through
- Sheds sitting on slabs or gravel with a gap around the base
- Precast or poured front steps with a hollow underneath
- Walkouts and additions where the backfill has sunk
- Window wells with no cover

Skunks can also do some damage of their own. Steady burrowing under a slab or a set of steps washes out the soil that supports it, and over time you can get cracking or settling.

## The kit calendar

If you take one thing from this page, make it this: when you find the skunk matters as much as where.

- **February and March.** Mating season. Males roam a long way at night, so you'll notice more spray smell around the neighbourhood.
- **April to June.** Babies are born, usually four to seven of them. They're born blind and deaf and depend on their mother for five to eight weeks.
- **May through July.** The nursing stretch. This is when you have to be careful. Block the hole or put a one-way door on it now, and the mother goes out for the night and can't get back in to the kits.
- **July and August.** The young start following mom out of the den, then head off on their own. By late summer they're independent.
- **September to November.** Skunks look for a warm winter den, often under a structure. They don't truly hibernate, but they sleep through cold spells and come out on milder nights.

That's why late summer into early fall, roughly August and September, is the easiest time for exclusion. The kits are mobile, and the ground is still soft enough to dig and bury mesh.

### What we do if there are kits

We don't seal anything until we know whether a mother is nursing under there. I'll listen at the opening at dusk for high, chattering squeaks, look for small tracks, and weigh it against the time of year.

If there are young, the plan changes. When the kits are big enough to follow her, we set things up so the mother can lead them out together, then we close it up. If they're still too small, we wait until they're old enough, usually by late July or into August, and keep you posted on the new timeline. Nobody gets left underground.

## Skunk removal in Vaughan, step by step

Here's how it actually goes when you book us.

1. **Walk the whole lot.** The hole you found is only the start. Deck perimeter, shed base, front and back steps, the AC pad, window wells, the foundation line. Skunks often dig a second exit, so I'm looking for every opening, active or old. I'm also looking at the soil, the grading and anything that might be undermined.
2. **Confirm it's a skunk and whether it's alone.** Tracks, digging, smell and the season tell me most of what I need.
3. **A written plan and quote.** What we're doing, where, and what it costs, before we start.
4. **Close the extra holes.** Secondary openings get blocked so the skunk is down to one way in and out.
5. **Put the one-way door on.** A flap or mesh door fixed tight over the main entrance. The skunk pushes out at night to eat and can't get back in. We don't bait it, we don't trap it, and nobody handles it.
6. **Watch for a few nights.** Usually three to seven. I'll smooth the dirt in front of the door so fresh tracks show which way the skunk went.
7. **Bury the mesh.** Once it's empty, we dig a trench around the deck, steps or shed, roughly 30 to 45 cm deep, attach heavy galvanized mesh to the base of the structure, run it down into the trench and bend the bottom out in an L. A skunk digging at the edge hits the mesh and gives up. Then we backfill and tidy the grade.
8. **Clean the den area.** Dig out the worst of the soil, remove droppings and bedding, and treat it with a disinfectant and an enzyme deodorizer.

From the first visit to a sealed perimeter, most jobs take about five to ten days. If there's concrete or masonry repair, it adds a few days. In kit season it can stretch into late summer.

### Front steps and slabs need extra care

Skunks under poured or precast steps, porches and garage slabs are a slightly different job. If you dig carelessly next to a slab, you can make the hollow worse. We dig carefully, keep the barrier tight to the concrete, and if the soil under it has really washed out, we'll tell you so and suggest bringing in a masonry or foundation contractor for that part.

A skunk wandering across your yard at night isn't an emergency. A few things are.

Call us right away for:

- **A skunk trapped** in a window well, a basement stairwell, a pool or an open trench
- **A strong smell coming into the basement or main floor,** which can mean a den right under the living space or an animal that died in a void
- **A den against the foundation or a support post** where the digging is undermining it
- **Repeated spraying** of kids or pets

And treat these as health calls first:

- **A skunk out in daytime that's staggering, circling or strangely bold.** That can be rabies or distemper. Keep people and pets away.
- **Any bite or scratch, or contact with saliva.** For a person, see a doctor and call [York Region Public Health](https://www.york.ca/health/infectious-diseases-and-immunization/rabies). For a pet, call your vet. Skunks are one of the main rabies carriers in Ontario.

Skunks can also carry leptospirosis, roundworm, fleas and ticks, which is one more reason not to have a den beside the kids' play area.

## If your dog takes a hit

Keep the dog outside if you can. Bringing a sprayed dog straight into the house spreads the oil onto carpets and furniture, and into the furnace returns.

Flush its eyes gently with clean water if they're red. For the coat, plain water just moves the oil around. A mix of 3% hydrogen peroxide, some baking soda and a squirt of dish soap works far better, or a skunk shampoo from your vet. Keep it out of the eyes, rinse it all off, and don't store any leftover mix in a sealed container. If your dog's eyes are still irritated the next day, or it's having trouble breathing, get it to the vet.

If the dog gets sprayed more than once, the skunk lives close by. Usually very close.

## Trapping and the one-kilometre rule

Ontario's [Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97f41) lets property owners deal with wildlife that's damaging their property, but with limits. If you live-trap a skunk, you have to release it within one kilometre of where you caught it. Body-gripping traps, leg-hold traps, glue boards and poison are all out.

So trapping doesn't buy you much. You risk getting sprayed, you might catch a neighbour's cat instead, and then you let the skunk go a few streets away and it walks home to the same open space under your steps. Exclusion skips all that.

The other home remedies don't hold up either:

- **Mothballs and ammonia rags.** Skunks shrug them off, and mothballs aren't meant to sit in your garden soil.
- **Lights and a radio.** Annoying for a night or two, then ignored.
- **Filling the hole with dirt or rocks.** In spring you can bury kits. Any other time, the skunk just digs a new entrance, often somewhere worse.
- **Poking around under the deck.** A skunk that feels cornered stamps its front feet and lifts its tail. That's your signal to back off slowly.

## When the smell hangs around

Sometimes the skunk's gone and the smell isn't. Skunk musk is oily and clings to anything porous, like insulation, carpet, drywall and the soil under the den, and it can travel through ductwork so it seems like the whole house smells.

Tonight, open windows, run the exhaust fans, and switch the furnace fan off for a bit so you're not pushing the smell through every room. Bowls of white vinegar or activated charcoal can take the edge off.

If it keeps coming back, especially on damp days, the source is still there. We track it down, which usually means the den soil or insulation right next to it, and treat it with enzyme products that break the musk down. Badly soaked insulation or carpet pad usually has to come out. In a closed-in crawlspace we sometimes bring in air scrubbers to clear what's left.

## Making your yard a worse place to live

Once the den is sealed, the rest is about being the least interesting yard on the street.

- **Bins in the garage** until collection morning, with lids that actually latch
- **Feed pets indoors** and sweep up seed under the feeder
- **Pick up fallen fruit** and keep compost in a closed bin
- **Deal with the grubs.** If your lawn is getting dug up, talk to a lawn care company about grub control
- **Clear out brush piles and stacked lumber** along the back fence
- **Cover window wells** and check deck skirting every spring
- **Building a new deck or shed?** Add the buried mesh while it's going in. It's much cheaper than doing it later.

## A few more questions

**Will the skunk spray during removal?**
Rarely. Nobody corners it. It walks out through the door on its own, at night, when it would have gone out anyway.

**Is the one-way door safe around my kids and dog?**
Yes. It's a passive door with no poison or chemicals, and we fix it so it can't be pulled off. The only digging is the trench along the structure, and we put the grade back afterward.

**Can I just wait for it to leave?**
It may wander off in fall, but if the space is still open, another skunk will find it. They're very good at that.

**Should I call pest control or a wildlife company?**
A wildlife company. Pest control mostly handles insects and mice. Skunks need exclusion work, and Vaughan requires wildlife removal companies to hold a city licence.
