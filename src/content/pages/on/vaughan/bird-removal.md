### Common Nuisance Bird Species in Vaughan

Several species account for the vast majority of bird removal calls across Vaughan. Many birds are protected under federal and provincial legislation, which makes species identification critical before any removal work begins. Inspection and species identification is the first step in professional bird control.

* **Rock pigeons (feral pigeons):** Year-round residents that nest on ledges, signage beams, parking garage rafters, and balcony corners. They form large roosting colonies and breed prolifically. Main problems include heavy droppings accumulation, corrosion of metal and masonry, and slip hazards on walkways.

* **House sparrows:** Favour cavity-like spaces-dryer vents, bathroom exhaust vents, gaps in soffits, and signage cavities. Active nesting typically runs from April through August. They spread quickly once established and can block airflow in vents.

* **European starlings:** Aggressive cavity nesters that target attic gable vents, open fascia ends, and HVAC penetrations. Winter roosting colonies on heated commercial buildings can number in the hundreds.

* **Seagulls (ring-billed and herring gulls):** Increasingly common on flat commercial roofs, parking lots near food courts, and institutional properties. Aggressive during nesting season (May–July), noisy, and prolific producers of droppings.

* **Canada geese:** Seasonal migrants that congregate around stormwater ponds, sports fields, and commercial lawns. Droppings foul walkways and entrances; nesting geese can be territorial and aggressive toward staff and customers.

* **Chimney swifts:** Occasionally encountered in older Vaughan homes with uncapped chimneys. A protected species requiring a careful approach-[active nests and eggs are protected by law](https://www.ontario.ca/page/remove-bird-nests-or-eggs), requiring special handling by companies.

Technical terms used throughout this article: a *roosting colony* is a group of birds that regularly returns to the same perch or sheltered area to rest overnight or through winter; a *nesting site* is a location where birds build nests and raise young; *flight paths* are the predictable routes birds take between feeding areas and roost or nest locations.

### Health, Fire, and Structural Risks of Bird Infestations

Bird infestations create far more than a nuisance. They introduce biohazards (disease-carrying organic matter), pathogens (microorganisms that cause illness), and fire hazards into homes and commercial buildings. Wildlife can cause significant property damage and health risks that escalate the longer they go unaddressed.

**Biohazard risks:**

* Bird droppings (guano) can carry fungal spores responsible for [histoplasmosis](https://www.publichealthontario.ca/en/Laboratory-Services/Test-Information-Index/Histoplasma-Culture-Identification), as well as bacteria such as salmonella and E. coli. Bird droppings can cause health issues such as histoplasmosis and E. coli. When dried droppings are disturbed, spores become airborne and can be inhaled.

* Accumulated guano in commercial loading bays, entryways, and walkways creates slip hazards and can trigger health code violations for food-service and retail businesses.

* Bird droppings can create stubborn messes and health hazards that require professional sanitation equipment to address safely.

**Fire and mechanical risks:**

* Nesting material-twigs, leaves, lint, feathers-packed into dryer vents is a serious fire risk. Lint-filled nests in dryer ducts can ignite from heat during normal operation.

* Blocked exhaust ducts reduce airflow, overwork HVAC fans, and in extreme cases can contribute to carbon monoxide buildup, especially in multi-unit condo buildings with shared ventilation.

**Structural and aesthetic damage:**

* The acidic composition of bird droppings corrodes metal railings, warehouse equipment, parapet walls, signage fixtures, and vehicle fleets parked beneath roosts.

* Droppings stain stone, stucco, and painted surfaces on plazas across Vaughan. Moisture trapped in nest material causes wood rot, and freeze-thaw cycles in Ontario winters expand cracks created by that trapped moisture.

**Indoor air quality:**

* Decomposing nests and carcasses inside wall voids, attic spaces, or ductwork produce foul odours and allergen-laden dust that can circulate through living and working spaces.

These risks underscore why professional bird removal-including thorough sanitation-is essential, not simply scaring birds to a different perch on the same building.

### Legal and Safety Reasons to Avoid DIY Bird Removal in Vaughan

Ontario and federal laws protect many wild bird species, and DIY attempts frequently cross legal lines-sometimes without the homeowner even realizing it.

**Legal constraints:**

* [The Migratory Birds Convention Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2022-105/section-63.html) protects many bird species during nesting seasons. Disturbing active nests containing eggs or chicks is illegal without a permit. Humane methods focus on exclusion rather than lethal removal of birds.

* Ontario's Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act and Endangered Species Act add additional layers of protection. Some species (house sparrows, starlings) have limited exemptions, but identifying which rules apply requires expertise.

* Homeowners remain personally liable if they violate protections or cause damage to neighbouring property during removal attempts.

* Municipal guidelines dictate that wildlife removal companies must have proper licenses. Companies must adhere to local licensing and insurance requirements for wildlife management. The City of Vaughan requires wildlife removal companies to hold a [Business Licence](https://www.vaughan.ca/business/business-licensing-and-permits/wildlife-removal-company-licence), carry at least CA$2 million in liability insurance, and provide written contracts before starting work.

**Safety risks:**

* Falls from ladders on two- and three-storey Vaughan homes are a leading cause of homeowner injury during attempted wildlife removal.

* Unprotected exposure to accumulated droppings releases airborne spores and bacteria. Without proper PPE (respirators, gloves, coveralls), cleanup itself becomes a health hazard.

* Improperly re-assembled vent covers, duct connections, or flue components after amateur nest removal can create fire hazards or carbon monoxide intrusion risks.

**Ineffectiveness of common DIY methods:**

* Plastic owls, reflective tape, and ultrasonic devices provide only brief relief. Pest birds habituate to visual and auditory deterrents within days.

* Physical barriers like bird spikes and netting are effective deterrents against nesting-but only when installed to professional specifications with no gaps in coverage. DIY installations commonly leave openings that birds exploit.

* Blocking vents without first clearing nests can trap birds inside, fully block airflow, and create a dead animal removal situation that worsens odours and contamination.

### Will the birds come back after you remove them?

Without proper exclusion, birds will almost certainly return to a site they have used before. That is why every Greater Wildlife Control project includes structural bird-proofing-vent covers, spikes, netting, gap sealing-designed to prevent re-entry permanently. Our warranty coverage and follow-up visits provide additional assurance. Long term protection depends on maintaining exclusion hardware and following the maintenance recommendations we provide.

### Can I clean up bird droppings myself after removal?

Small amounts of droppings on an exterior surface like a patio or driveway can be cleaned with gloves, a mask, and a wet-cleaning method that minimizes dust. However, large accumulations-in attics, inside vents, on warehouse floors, or across flat roofs-require professional biohazard sanitation with HEPA-filtered vacuums, hospital-grade disinfectants, and proper PPE. Disturbing large quantities of dried guano without respiratory protection risks serious health issues including histoplasmosis. When in doubt, rely on professional sanitation to protect yourself and your property.
