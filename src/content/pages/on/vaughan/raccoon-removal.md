A lot of people in Vaughan figure raccoons are an old-house problem. Then one rips the ridge vent off a home that's barely ten years old, and they're standing on the lawn trying to work out how it even got up there. We get that call all the time, and it's a big part of why raccoon removal in Vaughan looks a little different from what most people expect.

Think about how this city is laid out. You've got whole subdivisions of newer homes with plastic roof vents and vinyl soffits, and a lot of them back right onto the Humber River valley, woodlots, hydro corridors and stormwater ponds. Raccoons sleep in that green space and walk into the neighbourhood every night to eat. Green bins, pet food, a barbecue that didn't get scraped, and then a warm attic behind a vent they can pry open with their hands. For a raccoon, that's a pretty good deal.

So let me walk you through it the way I would if we were standing in your driveway: how to tell it's actually a raccoon, how they're getting onto your roof, why spring changes the whole job, what we do once we're on site, the cleanup nobody likes to think about, and how we close the house so you're not dealing with this again next year.

## What a raccoon in the attic actually sounds like

Raccoons work nights. The noise usually starts after dark when the animal heads out to feed, then picks up again in the early hours when it comes home. And it's heavy. People describe thumping, something that sounds like a person crawling, and sometimes a low growl or a churring noise. A squirrel is a daytime animal and a lot lighter on its feet. Mice are a faint, steady scratch inside the walls.

In spring, listen for babies. Raccoon kits make a high, chittering sound, and it tends to come from one spot near the eaves and go on and off through the day while mom sleeps.

Here's what else I'd look for:

- Droppings about ¾ of an inch thick and 2 to 3 inches long, way bigger than anything a squirrel leaves, and usually piled in one place
- A musky, ammonia smell near the attic hatch or in an upstairs closet
- Yellow or brown stains on the ceiling drywall from urine soaking through
- Cold spots or drafts upstairs where insulation has been flattened or dragged around
- Outside, a bent or missing roof vent, a soffit panel pushed up at the corner, lifted shingles along the edge, or muddy paw prints on a downspout
- Lights flickering or a breaker tripping around the same time you hear the noise, which can mean chewed wiring

That last one, I'd call about right away. Same goes if a raccoon has actually made it into the living space, or it's acting strangely around your kids or your dog. Keep everyone back and give us a call.

## How raccoons get up on a Vaughan roof

When people picture a raccoon climbing onto a house, they picture a big tree hanging over the roof. On the older streets in Woodbridge, sure, that happens. But plenty of newer lots in Vellore Village and Patterson don't have a single mature tree, and it doesn't slow raccoons down one bit. They'll go up a downspout, the corner of the brick, a fence post next to the garage, or the stone veneer around the front entry. Once they're on a lower roof, the rest is a stroll.

I was at a house in Patterson where the owners were sure nothing could reach their roof. No trees, nothing touching the house, so they figured it had to be squirrels. Then we found a muddy smear on the downspout at the corner of the attached garage, prints across the garage shingles, and a gap behind the dormer where the flashing met the vinyl siding. That raccoon was climbing the downspout, walking up the garage roof and slipping in behind the dormer every night. You'd never have seen that route from the backyard.

Once they're up there, these are the spots they test:

- **Plastic box vents.** The square vents near the peak on most newer homes. They're light and held down with a few nails, so a raccoon grabs the lip and pries.
- **Ridge vents.** The long vent along the very top of the roof. On a lot of new builds there's plastic under the cap shingles, and they'll tear into it.
- **Vinyl and aluminum soffit.** It's flexible. Pushing up on a panel at the corner is often all it takes.
- **Roof-to-wall joints.** Where a lower roof runs into a second-storey wall, around dormers, and over attached garages.
- **Plumbing stacks and exhaust vents.** Any gap around the base gets chewed and pulled wider.
- **Chimneys** with no cap, or a flimsy one, which we see more on the older homes in Woodbridge.

Older houses have their own list. Wood fascia and soffit that's gone soft after decades of weather, mortar gaps on brick where utility lines come through, and metal vents that have rusted thin. And don't forget ground level. Big Kleinburg decks with nothing screening the underside are a favourite place to den.

## Spring kits, summer explorers and fall lodgers

Raccoons mate in late winter. The kits arrive anywhere from about March into June, and they stay in the den for roughly 8 to 12 weeks before they can follow mom around. Put that together and you can have helpless babies in an attic from spring right through to midsummer.

That's why, between March and July, we go into every raccoon job expecting a litter. We don't put a door on anything until we've looked. If you seal a hole, or trap the adult, while kits are inside, they die up there. You get the smell, the flies, a much nastier cleanup, and you've broken Ontario's wildlife rules on top of it.

A family in Vellore Village had a roofer screw a new plastic vent down where the old one had been pulled off. Two nights later the raccoon was back, this time tearing open the ridge vent about a metre higher up. When we got into the attic, there was a litter of kits tucked into the insulation right under the ridge. That's why she was so determined to get back in, and it's why patching the hole she was using wasn't going to solve anything.

By summer, the young ones are out and about, poking at vents and soffits on their own, so we see a fresh round of damage in June, July and August. Then September through November is house-hunting season. Raccoons are looking for somewhere to spend the winter, and an attic with a soft spot gets picked fast.

Winter is quieter, but they don't truly hibernate. They sleep through the coldest stretches and come and go on milder nights through the same hole. If one is holed up in your attic in January, we'll talk through timing with you, because pushing an animal out in a deep freeze only makes sense when there's a real reason, like chewed wiring.

## Raccoon removal in Vaughan, step by step

### We start on the roof, then go into the attic

I walk the whole roofline first, on ladders and on the roof itself with a harness when the pitch calls for it. Every opening, used or not, gets photographed. Then I go into the attic if there's safe access, wearing a P100 respirator and coveralls, and look at the insulation. You can usually see trails where the raccoon has been walking, the latrine spot, the nest, and any chewed wires or ductwork.

You'll see the photos and get a written plan before we do any work. The price is built from what we found: how many openings need sealing, whether there are kits, and how much cleanup there is.

### Checking for kits

We listen, we look through the insulation near the entry, and if we see the female we check whether she's nursing. If the timing fits and the signs are there, we plan around a litter.

### When there are babies up there

We lift the kits out by hand, with heavy gloves on, and put them in a warmed, ventilated box right beside the entry hole. Mom comes back, finds them by scent and sound, and moves them to a new den herself, usually within 24 to 48 hours. It's calmer for everybody, the raccoons included, than trying to catch her. Vaughan's own [wildlife page](https://www.vaughan.ca/residential/animal-services/wildlife) asks removal companies to give families the chance to reunite like this.

### The one-way door

Once there are no dependent babies inside, a steel one-way door goes over the main entry. The raccoon can push out to go feed, but the door won't let it back in. Before that door goes on, we close up the other weak spots on the roof, so she can't just walk over and tear a new hole two metres away.

The door stays up for several nights, usually 3 to 5, longer if we have any doubt. When it's quiet, the door comes off and that last opening gets sealed permanently.

### Decks, porches and sheds

Raccoons under a deck get the same idea at ground level. We fit a one-way door at the hole they're using, wait for them to leave, then wrap the base in steel mesh that's bent outward and buried, so the next raccoon that tries to dig under hits metal.

## What Ontario law says about moving a raccoon

Ontario's [Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97f41/v15) lets a homeowner, or someone working for them, deal with a raccoon that's damaging the property. But there are conditions. If you live-capture one, it has to be released within 24 hours and within 1 km of where you caught it, in similar habitat, and you need the landowner's okay to release it on private land. Poison and glue traps are off the table, and we don't use body-gripping traps either.

So catching it and dropping it off at a conservation area across town isn't allowed. It also doesn't work. A kilometre is nothing to a raccoon, the hole is still open, and if it was a mother, her kits are now starving in your insulation.

We treat live trapping as a last resort, for situations like a raccoon that's ended up inside the house where a one-way door won't help. The rest of the time, it's eviction and sealing.

## The latrine is the part I worry about

Raccoons pick a toilet spot and keep going back to it. Over a few months that becomes a real pile, and it's the reason I tell people not to go poking around up there.

Raccoon droppings can carry the eggs of a roundworm called *Baylisascaris procyonis*. The eggs become infectious a few weeks after they're passed and can last for years. If you disturb the pile, that dust gets into the air, and swallowing or breathing it in can cause serious neurological illness. Raccoon urine and droppings can also carry leptospirosis and salmonella, and rabies is always a concern with a bite.

Here's how we handle it:

- We mist the droppings lightly to keep the dust down, then scoop them out and double-bag them.
- HEPA vacuums pick up the fine material. A regular vacuum spreads it.
- Insulation that's soaked with urine or sitting under the latrine comes out, along with a margin of about 30 cm around it. Lightly soiled areas can sometimes stay, depending on what we see.
- Joists, sheathing and the tops of the drywall get treated with disinfectant aimed at bacteria and parasites.
- For lingering smell, we use enzyme cleaners and fogging, and ozone in the worst cases.
- If there are ducts or a furnace near the mess, we cover them, clean the registers and returns, and swap the furnace filter so nothing gets pulled into the house.

We take before-and-after photos and write down what was removed and what was applied. Keep that paperwork. Most Ontario home insurance policies won't pay for the removal itself, but some do cover damage that came out of it, like ruined insulation, wiring or ceiling repairs, and your insurer will want to see it. A future buyer's home inspector might too.

You can stay in the house through all of this. We seal off the work area and keep it contained to the attic.

## Closing up the house for good

Honestly, the sealing is what decides whether you ever see us again.

Every opening on the inspection list gets closed, not only the one the raccoon was using. We use galvanized steel mesh in ¼ or ½ inch, metal flashing, steel vent covers and proper chimney caps, and we fasten everything into the framing. Screwing into shingles or vinyl alone won't hold. Raccoons are strong. Chicken wire, spray foam, duct tape and plastic covers are basically a suggestion to them.

On a newer Vaughan home, that often means swapping plastic box vents for steel guards, reinforcing the ridge vent, and backing the soffit corners with metal. On an older Woodbridge house, it's usually replacing rotted fascia and soffit and closing gaps around chimneys and utility lines. Either way, we try to make the repairs blend into the trim so your roofline doesn't look patched. Ask us what the warranty covers when we give you the price, and we'll walk you through it.

Then there's your side of it:

- Keep green bins and garbage locked or in the garage until the morning of pickup.
- Feed pets inside, and don't leave bowls on the deck overnight.
- Clean up under bird feeders, and pick up fallen fruit.
- Cut back any branches that reach toward the roof, by at least a couple of metres.
- Have the roof checked in late winter, before kit season starts. If you back onto a ravine or the valley lands, it's worth doing every year.

## A few things people ask me

**How long does the whole thing take?** The one-way door is usually up for 3 to 5 nights. If there are kits, add a day or two for mom to move them. With sealing and cleanup, most jobs wrap up in a few days to a couple of weeks, depending on the house and how much damage there is.

**What should I do tonight if I'm hearing it right now?** Don't go up there and don't block the hole. Keep kids and pets away from the attic hatch, write down when you hear the noise, and lock up the bins. Then call us and we'll set up an inspection.
