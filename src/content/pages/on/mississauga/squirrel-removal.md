## Understanding Squirrel Infestations and Behavior Patterns

A squirrel infestation occurs when one or more squirrels establish nesting sites inside a residential or commercial structure-typically in attics, wall voids, or roofline cavities. In Mississauga, the two most common wildlife species responsible are the [Eastern Grey Squirrel](https://www.mississauga.ca/services-and-programs/animals-and-pets/wildlife-in-mississauga/squirrels/) (which includes the melanistic "black" variant) and the smaller American Red Squirrel. Grey squirrels can reach 30–36 inches in total length including their tail, while red squirrels are considerably smaller at roughly 12 inches.

The impact on your property goes beyond nuisance noise. Squirrels chew electrical wiring, creating fire hazards. They soil insulation with droppings and urine, reducing its R-value and introducing allergens, parasites like fleas and ticks, and potential mold growth. Over time, their gnawing weakens soffits, roof vents, and structural wood, leading to costly damage that escalates the longer it goes unaddressed. The goal of humane removal is to resolve the underlying problem of pest intrusion-not just chase animals away temporarily.

Professional intervention matters because missed entry points or improper sealing can trap animals inside or lead to immediate re-entry. Experts bring technical tools like thermal imaging to detect hidden nests in wall cavities, conduct full wiring assessments, and ensure compliance with [Ontario wildlife regulations](https://www.ontario.ca/page/harass-capture-or-kill-wild-animal-damaging-private-property) that limit how squirrels can be captured and relocated. Attempting to handle wildlife problems without this expertise puts your home and health at risk.

### Seasonal Nesting Habits in Mississauga

Squirrels in Mississauga follow predictable breeding cycles tied directly to the region's humid continental climate. Spring breeding produces the first litters in March through April, when female squirrels actively seek sheltered spaces-your attic being an ideal candidate. A second litter typically arrives in July through August. During both periods, females pull fur from their shoulders and body to line nests, a clear behavioral indicator of active breeding.

Fall preparation from September through November brings another surge in activity. As temperatures drop toward Mississauga's mean January low of around −5°C, squirrels aggressively collect nesting materials and force entry into roof spaces to secure warm shelter for winter. This seasonal cycle means demand for wildlife removal services peaks in early spring-when new litters create urgent situations-and again in early fall as animals settle in before cold weather arrives.

Understanding these patterns helps homeowners identify the best timing for a thorough inspection. If you schedule a professional assessment before peak nesting seasons, you can prevent infestations rather than react to them.

### Common Entry Points and Structural Vulnerabilities

Identifying entry points is crucial for effective squirrel control. The most common structural vulnerabilities include gaps in soffits and fascia boards, unscreened roof vents, ridge vents, and gable vents. Missing or damaged chimney caps, cracks where the roof meets the wall, and gaps under shingles or along eaves all serve as open invitations. Wildlife proofing involves identifying weak entry points before animals exploit them.

Property age plays a significant role in susceptibility. Many Mississauga neighborhoods feature homes built in the mid-20th century with aging wood trim, loose soffit boards, and unsealed ventilation openings. These older structures often have multiple rooflines, dormers, and architectural details that create additional vulnerable areas and complicate inspection. Newer homes aren't immune either-lightweight construction materials can be easier for squirrels to chew through.

Understanding these vulnerabilities is exactly why a professional assessment matters: it's the foundation of every effective exclusion strategy that Greater Wildlife Control deploys.

### Humane One-Way Door Systems

One-way doors are the cornerstone of humane squirrel removal. These exclusion devices consist of a spring-loaded or flap-style metal door installed directly over the primary active entry point. Constructed from durable galvanized steel or heavy-gauge mesh, the mechanism allows squirrels to push through and exit but prevents them from pushing back in to re enter the structure.

Proper installation is critical. Before putting the one-way door in place, technicians must first seal all secondary openings so that squirrels are funneled toward the single equipped exit. The door's dimensions are sized to the species-grey squirrels require larger openings than reds. Flanges or collars around the device ensure a secure seal against the building surface, eliminating gaps that animals might exploit. Humane methods are essential for safe squirrel removal, and one-way doors represent the most humane way to humanely remove squirrels without causing stress or harm.

## Detailed Removal Process and Biohazard Sanitation

### Step-by-Step Removal Procedure

Greater Wildlife Control initiates this comprehensive process whenever an active squirrel infestation is confirmed or strongly suspected based on homeowner reports of sounds, sightings, or visible damage.

1. **Initial property inspection**: Technicians conduct a visual and thermal imaging survey of the attic, roofline, soffits, and exterior walls to identify active nesting sites, species present, and all entry and exit points.

2. **Entry point mapping**: Every structural weakness is cataloged-vents, cracks, roofline gaps, soffit openings, chimney areas-creating a complete vulnerability profile of the property.

3. **Secondary entrance sealing**: All identified entry points except the primary active opening are sealed with galvanized steel mesh, sheet metal, or hardware cloth to funnel squirrels toward a single exit.

4. **One-way exclusion device installation**: The exclusion door is installed at the primary access point, allowing squirrels to leave but not re enter.

5. **Monitoring period**: The device remains in place for 3–7 days (longer in cold or wet conditions). Technicians return to check for signs of continued activity-sounds, fresh droppings, or sightings-to ensure complete evacuation of all family units.

6. **Contaminated material removal**: Soiled insulation, nesting debris, droppings, and urine-stained materials are carefully bagged and removed for proper disposal.

7. **Professional sanitation**: The attic space is treated with professional-grade disinfectants, HEPA-vacuumed to remove allergens and particulates, and deodorized to eliminate pheromone trails that could attract new animals.

8. **Permanent exclusion barrier installation**: All openings-including the former one-way door location-receive permanent barriers using galvanized steel mesh, metal flashing, and stainless or galvanized fasteners. Chimney caps are installed or replaced as needed.

## Common Squirrel Removal Challenges and Solutions

Even with the right approach, squirrel removal presents complications that require professional expertise to navigate safely. Attempting to handle these situations without proper training and equipment often leads to costly mistakes, continued infestations, or legal issues.

### Multiple Entry Points and Hidden Damage

Many Mississauga homes-particularly older houses with complex rooflines, dormers, and multiple additions-have several unrecognized entry points. Squirrels exploit gaps in gutters, unscreened vents, loose roof flashing, and openings under eaves that aren't visible from ground level. Covering entry points is essential for effective wildlife proofing, but you first have to find them all.

Greater Wildlife Control uses thermal imaging and borescopes to identify internal wall entry, hidden nests in crawlspaces, and wiring damage inside wall cavities that isn't visible during a standard visual inspection. If even one secondary entry point is missed, squirrels will find it and return, rendering the entire exclusion effort ineffective.

### Mother Squirrels with Dependent Young

Ethical removal protocols require special care during breeding seasons. With two litters per year-spring (March–April) and summer (July–August)-there's a significant window during which young squirrels may be present in the nest but too small to follow their mother through a one-way door. Exclusion devices installed too early can orphan dependent young, leading to animal suffering and, ironically, new problems as trapped juveniles die inside wall cavities.

Professional technicians physically inspect nests before installing exclusion devices. If dependent young are found, removal is delayed until they're mobile enough to exit on their own. This approach balances effective property protection with responsible wildlife care.

### Contaminated Insulation and Health Hazards

Attic insulation contaminated by squirrel habitation acts as both a nesting substrate and a waste repository. Over time, accumulated droppings, urine, and parasites degrade the insulation's thermal performance, increase moisture retention that promotes mold growth, and create genuine health hazards from airborne particulates.

Professional-grade sanitization requires full PPE-respirators rated for biological particulates, disposable coveralls, and sealed containment during removal. Contaminated insulation is bagged and disposed of under local code. Only after complete cleaning and disinfection is new insulation installed, restoring the attic's energy efficiency and eliminating health risks. Regular wildlife proofing can keep animals off your property and prevent this kind of damage from recurring. Professional proofing services can ensure long-term wildlife prevention through durable materials and seasonal monitoring.
