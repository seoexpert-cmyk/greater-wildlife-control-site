Stand in your yard on a fall morning in Mineola or Port Credit and give it ten minutes. There's a good chance you'll see a squirrel run the full length of your eavestrough like it owns the place. Most of the time it's only passing through. When it starts stopping at the same corner of the roof every day, that's usually when people call us about squirrel removal in Mississauga.

The south end of the city has a lot of mature trees: big oaks, maples and walnuts that have been growing over these streets for decades. That's great for shade and great for squirrels. North of the 403, the trees are younger, but the houses have plenty of plastic vents and vinyl soffit, and a squirrel doesn't need a branch when it can climb a downspout or the brick corner.

I'll take you through what I look for, how squirrels get into houses here, why we check for babies first, how we get them out without a trap, and what you can do so they don't come back.

## What gives a squirrel away

Mostly, it's the clock. Grey squirrels are up with the sun. You'll hear quick scratching, running or a rolling sound in the morning, maybe again in the late afternoon, and then nothing overnight. Heavy movement after dark is more likely a raccoon. Faint scratching inside the walls at night tends to be mice.

The sound also tends to come from the edges of the room, not the middle. Squirrels like to nest near their way in, down where the roof meets the outside wall, so people hear them above a closet or along one side of a bedroom.

Other clues:

- A chewed hole with rough edges in a vent, a soffit corner or the fascia board
- Wood chips or slivers on the deck, driveway or patio under the roofline
- Leaves and twigs stuffed into the eavestrough near a vent
- Small, dark droppings near the attic hatch
- Insulation pulled into a pile mixed with leaves and bark
- Squeaking in late winter or midsummer, when there are babies

## Black squirrels are grey squirrels, by the way

People are often surprised by this one. The black squirrels you see all over Mississauga are Eastern grey squirrels, just a darker colour. Same habits, same appetite for your soffit. The city's [squirrel page](https://www.mississauga.ca/services-and-programs/animals-and-pets/wildlife-in-mississauga/squirrels/) has a bit more on them.

Red squirrels are smaller, louder and more territorial. We see them more on lots that back onto the Credit River valley or the wooded parts of Riverwood and Erindale. They'll stash food in the insulation, and they sometimes keep a few small holes going instead of one big one, which means more spots to find and seal.

## The spots they chew on Mississauga houses

A squirrel's front teeth never stop growing, so it chews all the time. If something on the roof is soft, loose or plastic, it gets tested. Here's where I check first:

- **Roof vents.** Plastic box and mushroom vents are the easy target. A squirrel can chew through the side of one in an afternoon.
- **Soffit corners and returns.** Where the soffit turns a corner or meets the brick, the panels pull apart over time, and that gap is a perfect starting point.
- **Behind the eavestrough.** When a gutter sags or pulls away from the fascia, there's a hidden gap between them. Squirrels slip in behind it and chew the wood where nobody can see.
- **Gable ends.** Especially where there's a gable vent, wood siding or cedar shakes.
- **Roof-to-wall joints** over garages and porches.
- **Chimneys** with no cap.

The older split-levels and backsplits in the south end tend to have wood fascia and gable trim that's been painted and repainted for years. Underneath, it can be soft. Newer townhouses in Churchill Meadows have the opposite problem: the materials are light, so there's not much for a squirrel to chew through.

We got a call from a house in Streetsville where the noise was only above the bedroom over the garage. From the ground, the roof looked fine. Once I was up the ladder, I could see the eavestrough at the garage corner had pulled away from the fascia by about a finger's width. The squirrel had squeezed in behind it, chewed the corner of the fascia, and walked straight into the garage attic, then over to the house side. Rehanging that gutter properly and putting steel behind it closed the whole thing off.

## Check for babies before anything gets sealed

Squirrels usually have two litters a year here. The first babies show up around March and April, and the second around July and August. Before those litters arrive, the female starts lining a nest, and an attic is about as cozy as it gets.

Young squirrels can't follow their mother for the first several weeks. If someone puts a one-way door on the hole while they're inside, mom is locked out and the babies starve in the insulation. You get the smell, the flies, and sometimes a mother squirrel chewing a brand new hole right beside the old one to get back to them. None of that is good for you or the squirrels.

So in spring and late summer, we look before we seal. If there are babies, we either wait until they're old enough to leave with her, or we lift them out by hand into a warmed box at the entry point so mom can move them to a tree nest herself. Which one makes sense depends on how old they are and where they are in the attic.

## Fall is moving season

The other rush comes from about September through November. Young squirrels from the summer litter are out looking for their own place, and the adults are hauling leaves and insulation into whatever warm spot they can find before winter. The calls we get from Cooksville and Malton in October are often brand new holes that weren't there in the summer.

Squirrels don't hibernate, either. They stay put on the coldest days, but they're active through most of the winter, so the noise doesn't stop just because there's snow on the roof. And every week they're up there, the nest gets bigger and the chewing keeps going.

If you only take one thing from this: a fresh hole in the fall is the cheapest one you'll ever fix. A squirrel that settles in for winter has months to spread out across the attic before the spring litter arrives.

## How squirrel removal in Mississauga works on our end

Here's the order we do things in:

1. **Talk it through on the phone.** When you hear it, where in the house, how old the home is, and whether you've seen anything outside. That usually tells me what animal it is and roughly where to look.
2. **Walk the roof.** Every vent, corner, gable, valley and gutter run. We take photos of all of it so you can see what we see.
3. **Check the attic** for nesting, droppings, and any sign of babies or chewed wiring.
4. **Seal the extra openings first.** Anything that isn't the main hole gets closed with steel mesh, flashing or a steel vent guard.
5. **Install a one-way door** on the main hole. The squirrel heads out on its morning run, the door closes behind it, and it can't get back in.
6. **Come back and confirm.** Most squirrels are out within a few days to a week. Once it's quiet and there's no fresh activity, the door comes off and the last hole gets closed permanently.

One thing I always mention: foam and caulk by themselves don't stop squirrels. They chew through foam like it isn't there. It has to be metal.

The other job I want to tell you about was in Mineola. The owners kept finding little reddish slivers of wood on the patio below the side of the house. The gable end was finished in cedar shakes, and a couple near the top had dried out and split. A squirrel had chewed at the split, worked its way behind the shakes, and gotten into the attic beside the gable vent. We put steel mesh in behind the area, replaced the damaged shakes so it matched from the street, and screened the gable vent while we were up there.

## What about a cage trap from the hardware store?

I understand the temptation. But in Ontario, a squirrel you trap has to be released within 24 hours and within 1 km of where you caught it. That's the rule under the Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act, and the province explains it on its page about [dealing with wild animals damaging your property](https://www.ontario.ca/page/harass-capture-or-kill-wild-animal-damaging-private-property). A kilometre is nothing for a squirrel. It's often back at your house within a day or two, and if the hole is still open, it goes right back in.

Traps also catch whatever wanders into them, like the neighbour's cat. And trapping a nursing mother leaves her babies stranded in the attic. We only use live capture when a one-way door won't work, for example when a squirrel has ended up loose inside the house.

Poison is a hard no. A poisoned squirrel crawls into a wall to die, and then you've got weeks of smell and a carcass nobody can reach. It's also a risk to pets and to the hawks that hunt along the creek valleys.

## Cleaning up after them

Squirrels aren't as messy as raccoons, but a nest that's been there a while still leaves droppings, urine-soaked insulation, and fleas or mites. Urine-damp insulation stops doing its job and can start to grow mould.

If it was a short stay in one corner, we take out the nest and droppings, HEPA vacuum the area and treat it. If they've been up there for months, the soiled insulation usually comes out and new insulation goes in. We wear respirators and coveralls for this, and we keep the dust from drifting down into the house.

The big thing I'd check is wiring. Squirrels chew on cables, and chewed wiring under insulation is a fire risk you won't see until it's a problem. If we find any, have an electrician look at it before new insulation goes over it.

## Small habits that keep them off the roof

Once everything is sealed, the house should stay closed. The repairs come with a warranty, and we'll go through what it includes before the work starts. A few things on your side help it last:

- Cut back branches so they're at least a couple of metres from the roof.
- Keep the eavestroughs clean and tight against the fascia.
- Go easy on bird feeders near the house, or move them out into the yard.
- Have a look at the roofline each spring and fall, especially the corners.
- If you're planning a new roof, ask for metal vents instead of plastic.

## Until we get there

- Don't plug the hole yourself. If babies are inside, or the squirrel is, you'll trap them in and the chewing will just move to a new spot.
- Keep a note of when you hear it and which room it's above. That helps me narrow it down fast.
- If you can see the hole from the ground, snap a photo.
- Keep the attic hatch closed, and keep pets out of any room where a squirrel could get down into the house.
- If a squirrel is loose inside, close the door to that room, open a window if it's safe to, and leave it alone.
