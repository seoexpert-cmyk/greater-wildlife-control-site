Some of the nicest houses in Oakville are the hardest ones to keep a raccoon out of. Walk the side streets off Lakeshore Road in Old Oakville or Bronte and you'll see deep wooden eaves, old brick chimneys, cedar trim, dormers on top of dormers, and big oaks and maples leaning over all of it. It's lovely to look at. To a raccoon, it's a building with a dozen soft spots, and that's where a good share of our raccoon removal in Oakville happens.

The rest happens up north. The newer streets in Palermo, Joshua Creek and Iroquois Ridge don't have old wood to tear at, but they have plastic roof vents, vinyl soffit and stone veneer that a raccoon can climb about as easily as you'd climb a ladder. And no matter which end of town you're in, you're never far from the Sixteen Mile Creek or Bronte Creek valleys. Raccoons sleep down there during the day and walk up into the neighbourhoods once it's dark.

So here's what I'd want you to know: what to look and listen for, where they get into houses here, how the time of year decides what we can and can't do, how a job actually runs on your roof, and roughly what it costs.

## What you'll notice first

Most people hear it before they see a thing. Raccoons are night animals, so the noise starts a little after sunset when one heads out to feed, then comes back in the small hours when it returns to sleep. It's a solid, heavy animal, and you can tell: slow footsteps, something being dragged, the occasional bump against a rafter, sometimes a low purring churr. Squirrels keep daytime hours and weigh a fraction as much. Mice are a faint scratch inside the wall.

Then take a look around. Outside, you might find:

- A roof vent knocked sideways or gone altogether
- A soffit panel bowed up at a corner, or wood trim torn open
- Shingles curled back along the lower edge of a roof
- Grey, greasy marks and a few stray hairs around a gap
- Scuffs or muddy prints on a downspout, the chimney or the stone beside the front door
- A ring of small holes in the lawn where something's been digging for grubs

Inside, it's usually a sharp, cat-litter kind of smell around the hatch or in an upstairs closet, or a brownish stain creeping across a ceiling. In spring, listen for a thin chirping that comes and goes during daylight hours while the mother sleeps. That's almost always kits.

If a raccoon gets into the living space, or you see one outside that's wobbling, circling or oddly calm around people, keep everyone back. A sick or injured animal is a job for the Town's animal services, run through the [Oakville & Milton Humane Society](https://www.oakville.ca/home-environment/pets-wildlife/). They don't take healthy raccoons out of attics, which is where we come in. And if anybody in the house gets bitten or scratched, call your doctor and Halton Region Public Health that same day.

## Why lakeside homes and new builds both get hit

### The older houses near the lake

The homes in Old Oakville, Bronte and along Lakeshore Road were built with a lot of wood on the outside. Years of wet wind coming off the lake soften that wood wherever water sits: the bottom edge of a fascia board, the corner where two soffits meet, the trim boxing in a dormer. A raccoon finds the soft spot, works its fingers in and pulls.

On these houses I check:

- **Masonry chimneys.** Plenty have no cap, or a cap that rusted through long ago. An unused flue is a dry, quiet den.
- **Dormers and valleys,** where the flashing has lifted.
- **Wood soffit and fascia,** especially on the shady north side where it never fully dries.
- **Additions,** where a newer roof was tied into an older wall and the joint was never quite right.
- **Overhanging trees.** A mature oak reaching over the roof gets a raccoon up there without any climbing on the house at all.

### North of Dundas

In Palermo, Joshua Creek, Iroquois Ridge and the newer pockets around them, the houses are tight but the roofs are finished in light materials. Plastic box vents near the peak are held down with a handful of nails. Vinyl soffit flexes. A lot of ridge vents have plastic under the cap shingles. And there's plenty to climb: downspouts, brick corners, stone around the entry, a fence that ends right next to the garage. Decks with open undersides are common too.

Somewhere in between are Clearview, Falgarwood and the other 1970s and 80s streets. You get a bit of both there: original aluminum vents gone brittle, wood fascia hiding under aluminum capping, and trees that have finally grown tall enough to reach the roof.

Not every raccoon comes in through the roof, though. One October I was at a house in Iroquois Ridge where the owners were sure there was one living up in the shingles. The roof was perfect. What we found was a garage door that stayed open most evenings, metal storage shelves against the back wall, and a ceiling hatch above them pushed off its frame. The raccoon had been wandering in, climbing the shelves and bedding down in the space over the garage. With no kits that late in the year, we waited until a camera showed it leaving at dusk, screwed the hatch shut and asked them to keep the door down after dark.

## The month you call changes the job

Raccoons mate in late winter. Most litters arrive in April or May, usually four or five kits, and the babies don't really get moving until they're about five weeks old. They stay with their mother well into summer.

So from about March through July, I walk into every attic expecting a family. Seal a hole or trap the adult during those months and the kits are left behind in the insulation, where they die. Then you've got the smell, the flies, a much bigger cleanup bill, and you've crossed Ontario's wildlife rules on top of it.

Summer gets noisy. The kits start clambering around, and by June and July there's a lot more traffic over your ceiling. Fall is when raccoons choose a winter spot, so from September through November we see fresh damage on houses that were fine in August.

Winter doesn't mean empty. Raccoons don't hibernate. In a hard cold snap they go into a slowed-down, sleepy state called torpor and stay put, then head out again on milder nights. An attic that's quiet in January can still be filling up with droppings. We'll talk about timing with you, because I don't like evicting an animal into the worst of February unless something can't wait, like a chewed cable or one finding its way into the house.

A family in Bronte called us one spring because they could hear chirping coming out of the living room fireplace. They hadn't lit it in years, the damper was shut, and they figured a bird had dropped down the flue. When I shone a light down from the roof, there was a mother raccoon curled on the smoke shelf with her kits tucked in behind her. We left the damper closed, kept the fireplace off and gave her a couple of nights to carry them out to another den, which she did. Then a stainless steel cap went on the chimney.

## Raccoon removal in Oakville, from the first visit to the last

### Up the ladder before anything else

Nothing gets fastened to your house until I've been over the whole roof. I'm counting every opening, used or not, working out which one the raccoon is actually going through, and looking for any sign of a litter. Then the attic, if the hatch and framing let me in safely, in a respirator and coveralls. The insulation tells you a lot: you can see the paths she walks, the corner she uses as a toilet, where the nest is and whether anything's been chewed.

You get photos of all of it and a written plan. The price comes straight out of that plan.

### When there's a litter

If there are kits, finding them comes first. Once we have them, we move them one at a time with gloved hands into an insulated box with a heat pack, and that box sits at the mouth of the opening she's been using. She'll come back after dark, track them down by their calls and her nose, and carry them off to a backup den she already knows. Usually that's done within a night or two. It's a lot calmer for everybody than trying to catch a protective mother in a cramped attic.

### The exit door

With no dependent kits inside, we mount a steel one-way door on the main entry. She can push out to go feed. She can't get back in. Before it goes on, we close the other weak spots on that roof, because a raccoon locked out of one hole will happily tear a new one a metre over.

The door has to be shaped to your roof. An old cedar-trimmed house in Old Oakville is a completely different fit from vinyl soffit in Palermo, and a door that doesn't sit tight just shows the animal where to start ripping.

It stays up for a few nights at least, longer if I have any doubt. We're usually back to check within three to seven days. After a stretch of silence we take the door down and close up the hole it was guarding.

### Chimneys

A raccoon in a chimney gets its own plan. Please don't try to smoke it out. Lighting a fire under a mother and her kits is cruel, and all that nesting material can start a chimney fire. We get the family out first, then cap the flue properly. If the crown or the flashing is cracked, we'll tell you while we're up there.

### Decks, porches and sheds

A lot of newer Oakville yards have decks skirted in lattice or thin vinyl, and raccoons love the dry space underneath. You'll usually see a round hole dug at the edge, packed dirt where they come and go, and a musky smell. The opening they use gets a one-way door, and after they've moved out we bury galvanized hardware cloth around the edge, 12 to 18 inches down, so the next one hits metal as soon as it starts digging.

## Moving a raccoon: what the province allows

Ontario's [Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97f41/v1) gives you, or whoever you bring in to help, the right to act when a raccoon is wrecking your house. What it doesn't do is let anyone haul that raccoon across Halton. Catch one alive and you've got 24 hours to let it go, at a spot no more than a kilometre from where it was caught. If that spot is somebody else's land, they have to say yes first. The province spells this out on its page about [wild animals damaging private property](https://www.ontario.ca/page/harass-capture-or-kill-wild-animal-damaging-private-property).

Even where trapping is allowed, it rarely solves anything. A raccoon let go at the end of your street knows the way home, and the gap in your soffit is still sitting there open. Trap a mother in May and her kits are still upstairs. Live capture is something we save for the strange ones, say a raccoon that has wandered down into a bedroom. Poison, drowning and glue are against the law, and we wouldn't touch them anyway.

## Cleaning the attic once they're gone

Raccoons pick one spot as a toilet and go back to it night after night. On a long stay that pile gets big, and it's the reason I'd ask you to keep out of the attic.

Raccoon scat often holds the eggs of *Baylisascaris procyonis*, a roundworm. Those eggs hold up for years in dust and soil, and if someone swallows or breathes them in they can do serious harm to the eyes and the nervous system. Small children are most at risk, mostly because of where their hands end up. Raccoons can also carry rabies, and distemper is a real danger to a dog that isn't vaccinated.

Cleanup goes roughly like this. We bag the latrine and the nesting material while keeping the dust down, then HEPA vacuum the fine stuff, since a household vacuum just throws it around the room. Insulation that's soaked or sitting under the latrine comes out. The joists and the roof deck get disinfected and deodorized. If a big patch of insulation had to go, we top it up or replace it, and if we spot chewed wiring, we'll point it out so an electrician can look before new insulation covers it.

Urine-soaked insulation stops doing its job and keeps on smelling, so after a full season up there it usually has to come out. If they were only there a few weeks, spot cleaning may do it. I'll show you the photos and we'll decide together.

## What does raccoon removal in Oakville cost?

It depends on the house. What moves the number:

- How high and steep the roof is, and how many levels it has
- How many openings need sealing. One soffit corner is a lot less work than a chimney, three vents and a deck.
- Whether there are kits, which means extra visits
- How much of the attic is soiled
- The season, since spring and fall are when we're busiest

As a rough guide, a simple job (one entry, no babies, light mess) usually falls somewhere between the low hundreds and the low thousands of dollars. A bigger house with several entries, a litter and soiled insulation can climb into several thousand over a few visits. Replacing insulation across the attic is priced on top, by the square footage and the type you pick.

My honest take: the cheapest raccoon job is the one you call about in the first week.

## Sealing the roof so it stays shut

The door gets the animal out. The sealing decides whether another one moves in next season. We close every gap we found, including the ones nothing is using yet. We use heavy-gauge galvanized mesh, metal flashing, steel vent guards and a proper chimney cap, all fastened into solid wood. Plastic screens, foam and thin aluminum won't stop a determined raccoon for long. On the older houses we often replace rotted fascia or soffit and match the trim, so the roof doesn't look patched from the sidewalk.

We back the exclusion work with a warranty. Ask me to go over its terms when I'm at the house. I'd much rather you know them before we start than find out later.

Your part is mostly about food and access. Keep the green bin and garbage behind a closed garage door until collection day, and bring pet food in at night. Take bird feeders down, or at least keep the spilled seed cleaned up. Get branches cut back so they don't touch the roof. And if you back onto one of the creek valleys, have someone look over the roofline every fall before raccoons start hunting for a winter den.

## Before we get there

Leave the hole alone for now. If kits are inside, blocking it is the worst thing you can do. Stay out of the attic and don't handle any droppings. Keep kids and pets clear of the hatch and of the spot outside where it's been coming and going. It helps if you jot down the times you hear it and roughly which room it's over, and if there's damage you can see from the yard, grab a photo.
