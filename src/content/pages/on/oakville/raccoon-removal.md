## Understanding Raccoon Infestations in Oakville Homes

A raccoon "infestation" is not a raccoon tipping your green bin on the curb. It means one or more raccoons have established a den inside your building - in the attic, within a wall cavity, inside a chimney flue, or beneath a deck or porch - and are using your property as shelter, a nesting site, or both. This distinction matters because surface-level nuisance behaviour (garbage raiding, roof walking) often precedes a structural intrusion but requires different solutions.

Oakville's geography makes raccoon intrusions especially common. The town's ravine systems, mature tree lines, and proximity to the Lake Ontario shoreline create ideal habitat corridors that channel raccoons directly into residential neighbourhoods. Local habitats with mature tree lines and proximity to water bodies attract raccoons in high numbers. Older homes in areas like Old Oakville and Bronte feature complex rooflines, aging soffits, and multiple dormers - structural characteristics that give raccoons easy access. Newer subdivisions with unfinished deck skirting and plastic-screened vents present different but equally exploitable vulnerabilities. In both cases, raccoons use attics, soffits, and chimneys as maternal dens in spring and as warm shelter during the colder months, directly causing property damage, contamination, and health risks for occupants.

### Raccoon Behaviour and Seasonal Nesting Habits

Understanding wildlife behaviour is essential to timing removal correctly. Raccoons (*Procyon lotor*) are nocturnal, highly intelligent, and physically powerful for their size. Their dexterous front paws can open containers, peel back flashing, and tear through rotting wood. They climb brick walls, downspouts, and vinyl siding with ease, and they prefer warm, dark, elevated spaces - which is why your attic is more appealing to them than a hollow tree.

Oakville-specific seasonality drives when and how raccoons enter homes:

* **Late winter to spring (February–April):** Females actively search for den sites as breeding begins. Most raccoons give birth in April or May, with an average litter of 4–5 kits. This is the most sensitive period for removal because separating a mother from dependent young is both illegal and inhumane. Spring is the busiest season for wildlife seeking nesting sites, and qualified wildlife control operators typically avoid trapping and relocating raccoons during baby season.

* **Summer (May–August):** Kits become mobile around five weeks old, dramatically increasing attic noise and roof traffic. Homeowners often first notice problems during this period.

* **Fall (September–November):** Pre-denning behaviour intensifies. Raccoons test rooflines, chimney crowns, and soffit gaps, scouting winter shelter. Juveniles that have not yet dispersed may still accompany their mother. Wildlife often seeks shelter in attics during colder months as temperatures begin to drop.

* **Winter (December–January):** Raccoons do not truly hibernate. They enter periods of torpor when temperatures drop below approximately −9 °C but wake during milder stretches to forage. A quiet attic in January does not mean an empty one - it often means worsening contamination and insulation damage accumulating unseen.

These seasonal patterns dictate the removal process. Mother-and-kits situations require specialized, humane handling - including hand-retrieval of babies and family reuniting protocols - while fall prevention work can stop the next winter's infestation before it begins.

### How Raccoons Enter Oakville Properties: Structural Vulnerabilities

Structural vulnerabilities are simply the weak spots and gaps in your building envelope that raccoons exploit to get inside. Animals can enter homes through gaps as small as a quarter, and raccoons can widen marginal openings quickly with their powerful forepaws.

Common entry points in Oakville homes include:

* **Roofline intersections and lifted shingles** - especially where dormers meet the main roof or where hip and ridge lines create construction gaps.

* **Soffit and fascia gaps** near eavestroughs, particularly in older homes where wood has rotted or frames have shifted.

* **Uncapped or poorly screened chimneys** - rusted or damaged chimney caps are one of the most frequent access points.

* **Roof vents, gable vents, and turbine vents** with flimsy plastic screens that raccoons chew or tear open in minutes.

* **Construction gaps around dormers** and where additions meet the original structure.

* **Damaged lattice and ground-level gaps** under decks, sheds, and porch steps.

This is precisely why a professional inspection is critical. Homeowners who spot one obvious hole and block it often miss two or three secondary potential entry points. Efficient raccoon removal involves performing thorough inspections to identify all possible entry points - not just the one with visible damage. Comprehensive property inspections utilize thermal imaging or physical checks to identify entry points that are invisible from ground level.

### Health Risks and Biohazard Concerns from Raccoons Indoors

The health risks from an indoor raccoon den extend well beyond the animals themselves. Raccoon feces may carry *Baylisascaris procyonis* roundworm - a parasite whose eggs can survive in soil and on surfaces for years and, in rare cases, cause permanent neurological damage or blindness in humans, particularly in children. Droppings and urine from wildlife can lead to serious health hazards, and raccoon feces accumulate rapidly in a confined attic space.

Rabies remains a real safety concern in Ontario. Raccoons accounted for the majority of rabid wildlife identified in the province between 1999 and 2003, with [outbreaks often clustering in fall and winter denning periods](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17092890/). Skunks can spread the rabies virus to pets and humans as well, making any direct contact with wild animals dangerous. Canine distemper is another serious viral disease carried by raccoons that can infect unvaccinated most dogs and cause permanent neurological damage - though it does not affect humans.

Raccoons leave behind contaminated insulation and waste that may create sanitation issues affecting the entire home. Urine-saturated insulation loses its thermal value, generates persistent ammonia odours, and degrades indoor air quality. Contaminated insulation can affect indoor air quality and health for everyone in the building. Nesting material, soiled batts, and torn vapour barriers compound the problem. Raccoons can also chew electrical wiring, creating fire hazards that many homeowners never discover until an electrician or wildlife professional inspects the attic.

Disturbing droppings or insulation without proper personal protective equipment (N95 respirator, gloves, coveralls) and containment procedures is unsafe. This is one of the strongest reasons why professional sanitation is an inseparable part of proper raccoon removal - it is not optional aftercare.

Understanding how and why raccoons move in helps explain what a professional removal process must include and why shortcuts create bigger problems.

### Step 2: Humane Removal Techniques for Adults and Kits

One-way doors are used for humane wildlife removal - they are the primary tool for raccoon eviction. These devices are installed directly over the main entry point and allow raccoons to push out through a spring-loaded flap but prevent them from re-entering. The design is simple and effective, and it lets the animal leave on its own schedule without the stress, injury risk, or legal complications of live trapping.

Mother-and-baby scenarios demand additional care. Humane eviction research emphasizes family reuniting protocols during active baby season. When kits are present in insulation or wall cavities, the process works as follows:

* Baby raccoons are hand-retrieved to ensure their survival when their mother is evicted. Baby raccoon protocols require hand-extracting newborns to prevent abandonment by the mother.

* Maternity reuniting and removal involves retrieving baby raccoons and placing them in a heated box positioned near the exit point.

* The mother raccoon, once she exits through the one-way door, locates her kits by sound and scent and carries them to an alternative den she already uses.

Humane methods include careful handling of baby animals at every stage. Humane removal services ensure no animals are harmed throughout the process.

Live trapping may still be used in specific situations - for example, a ground-level nuisance animal or a structure where a one-way door cannot be safely installed - but wildlife removal must comply with local protection laws. Under Ontario's FWCA, [captured wildlife must be released within 1 kilometre of the capture site](https://www.ontario.ca/page/harass-capture-or-kill-wild-animal-damaging-private-property), and only on property where the landowner has given permission. Poisoning, drowning, and unregulated lethal methods are illegal and inhumane, and they create additional problems including dead animal odours, secondary poisoning of other animals, and legal penalties.

### Step 3: Post-Removal Sanitation, Repairs, and Exclusion

Getting raccoons out is only half the job. Without sanitation and permanent sealing, odours persist, biohazards remain, and the scent trail left behind attracts new unwanted visitors to the same entry points. Professional services help prevent future wildlife re-entry through a three-part process:

* **Insulation repair or replacement:** Where insulation is heavily soiled, compressed, or urine-saturated, it is removed and replaced. This restores thermal performance and eliminates lingering odour.

* **Permanent sealing and structural exclusion:** Structural exclusion uses heavy-duty materials to secure vulnerable areas against raccoon entry - heavy-gauge galvanized steel mesh, metal flashing, aluminum capping, and proper chimney caps. These materials resist chewing, prying, and weather far better than plastic screens or expanding foam.

## Common Raccoon Problems in Oakville and How to Solve Them

Greater Wildlife Control's most frequent calls in Oakville fall into three categories: raccoons in attics near ravines and waterfront areas, raccoons under decks in newer subdivisions, and raccoons in chimneys and on roofs of older homes in neighbourhoods like Old Oakville and Bronte.

### Raccoons in the Attic or Walls

**Typical signs:** Heavy nighttime thumping or scratching, chattering or vocal sounds in early spring (indicating kits), insulation disturbed or visible near attic hatches, stains on ceilings below the attic, and a persistent musty or ammonia-like smell. Animals can cause significant property damage and health risks when left unaddressed for even a few weeks.

**What to do:**

* **Immediate homeowner steps:** Do not enter the attic at night. Keep household pets and children away from suspected den areas. Do not block holes - you risk trapping animals inside, which creates worse structural damage, odour, and legal issues.

* **Professional response:** Rapid inspection to confirm species and count, one-way door installation, kit rescue and reunion when present, followed by permanent sealing and full cleanup. Squirrel removal follows a similar exclusion process if squirrels are identified instead; squirrels' teeth grow 6 inches every year, allowing them to chew through materials that would stop raccoons, so species-specific strategies matter. Squirrels can carry bacteria and pathogens harmful to humans as well.

### Raccoons Under Decks, Sheds, and Porches

Raccoons frequently dig small round holes and use low-clearance voids beneath decks and sheds as ground-level dens, especially in newer Oakville developments where deck skirting is unprotected lattice or thin vinyl.

**Signs to watch for:** Soil disturbance and small round holes near the deck perimeter, entry tunnels with compacted soil, strong musky odour, and daytime sightings of raccoons entering or exiting.

**Solution steps:**

* Humane eviction with one-way doors installed at the identified entry points.

* Trenching and burying heavy-gauge wire mesh (hardware cloth) around the deck perimeter to a depth of 12–18 inches to exclude future digging. This provides permanent prevention services against ground-level re-entry.

* Monitoring to confirm vacancy before final sealing.

### Raccoons Raiding Garbage, Roofs, and Backyard Features

Surface-level nuisance issues - tipped garbage and green bins, damage to shingles when raccoons access the roof, raccoons on patios or in pools - are the most visible part of a raccoon problem but often signal that a structural intrusion is imminent or already underway.

**Practical solutions:**

* Use wildlife-resistant bins and secure compost containers. Remove bird feeders or bring them in at night, as these attract raccoons and other animals including birds, skunks, and rodents.

* Trim overhanging branches that act as "bridges" to roofs - raccoons routinely use tree limbs to bypass ground-level barriers.

* Install chimney caps and replace flimsy vent screens with heavy-gauge metal alternatives. These steps also reduce entry by squirrels and birds.

While these are effective DIY prevention steps, if raccoons are already inside the structure, professional exclusion and pest removal are needed. Pest control for insects, insect control, and rodents are separate services; raccoon removal requires wildlife-specific expertise, not a general exterminator.

**Glossary of key terms used in this article:**

* **Exclusion:** The practice of sealing a building to prevent wildlife from entering or re-entering, using durable materials installed at all identified access points.

* **One-way door:** A device installed over a wildlife entry point that permits animals to exit but blocks re-entry, allowing humane eviction without trapping.

* **Biohazard sanitation:** Professional cleaning, disinfection, and material removal in areas contaminated by raccoon feces, urine, and nesting debris.

* **Construction gap:** An unintended opening in the building envelope where different structural elements meet - such as where a dormer joins the main roof - that wildlife can exploit for access.

* **Torpor:** A state of reduced metabolic activity that raccoons enter during extreme cold; not true hibernation, as raccoons wake and forage during warmer periods throughout winter.

* **Furbearing mammal:** A legal classification under Ontario's FWCA that includes raccoons and governs how they may be trapped, handled, or have their dens disturbed.

* **Reunion box:** A heated, enclosed container used to hold hand-retrieved baby raccoons near the den exit so their mother can safely relocate them after eviction.
