A skunk living under your deck usually lets you know in one of two ways. Either there's a musky, burnt-rubber smell in the basement that wasn't there last week, or you walk out one morning and the lawn is covered in little cone-shaped divots, like someone went over it with an apple corer. That's a skunk digging for grubs.

Skunk removal in Toronto is a big part of what we do from early spring through fall. The good news is that skunks are pretty predictable. They want a dark, dry hole near food, and they'll leave if you give them a way out and take the hole away. The bad news is they won't leave on their own, and most of the tricks people try first, like mothballs, ammonia or a shovel full of dirt in the hole, either don't work or make things worse.

I'll go through it the way I would in your back yard: how to tell it's really a skunk, why spring jobs look different from fall ones, what to do when the dog gets sprayed, and how we get them out and keep them out.

## Is it really a skunk under there?

Raccoons and groundhogs both like decks and sheds too, so it's worth knowing the difference before you do anything.

Skunk signs:

- **Small, cone-shaped holes in the lawn,** a couple of centimetres across, mostly overnight. Raccoons tend to roll the sod back in strips instead.
- **A burrow entrance about the size of a grapefruit** along the edge of a deck, a shed or a porch step, with loose soil pushed out in front.
- **A musky smell** near the opening, even if nobody's been sprayed. Skunks just smell like skunk.
- **Tracks** in soft dirt with five toes and long front claws.
- **Activity at night.** Skunks are mostly out from dusk to dawn.

Groundhogs dig bigger holes with a big pile of dirt out front, and they're out during the day. Raccoons are more likely to be in the attic or the chimney than under a deck, though they'll use both.

If you aren't sure, a trail camera pointed at the hole for a night or two will settle it. Or take a photo and send it to us.

## Why Toronto yards suit skunks so well

The striped skunk does great in this city. Look at a typical older lot in Bloor West Village or the Junction and you'll see why:

- Decks with no skirting, just open space between the frame and the dirt
- Sheds sitting on patio stones or gravel with a gap underneath
- Concrete front porches where the soil has washed out from under the slab
- Window wells without covers
- Old additions and utility trenches that were never properly backfilled

Then there's the food. Lawns full of grubs, compost bins, pet food left on the back step, bird seed under the feeder, fallen fruit. And the ravines and rail corridors give skunks a highway into the neighbourhood. Houses backing onto the Humber get plenty of visitors.

Skunks don't need much space. A gap a bit bigger than a softball under the edge of a shed is enough. And they're strong diggers with long claws, so if there's soft soil next to a gap, they'll make the gap bigger.

## What time of year is it? That changes everything

Skunks have a pretty fixed calendar in southern Ontario.

- **February and March:** Breeding. You'll sometimes smell more spray around this time as males wander and fight.
- **April and May:** Babies are born, usually a litter of five or six. They're blind and helpless at first and open their eyes at around three weeks.
- **May and June:** Our busiest skunk season. Mothers are denning with kits under decks and sheds all over the city.
- **June and July:** The kits start following mom out of the den at about six to eight weeks old. You'll sometimes see a line of little skunks trailing behind her across the yard at dusk.
- **Late summer and fall:** Skunks eat everything they can find to put on weight for winter. More digging, more yard visits, more run-ins with dogs.
- **Winter:** They don't truly hibernate, but they hole up and sleep through the cold stretches, often deep under a structure.

Why does it matter? Because if you block a hole in May, there's a good chance you're shutting babies inside or cutting them off from their mother. That's cruel, it's against Ontario rules on how you're allowed to deal with wildlife on your property, and in a week or two you'll know about it from the smell. So spring jobs get a different plan, which I'll get to below.

If it happens to you, here's what to do:

1. **Rinse the eyes first** with plain, clean water. Spray in the eyes stings and can cause temporary redness and squinting.
2. **Wash with the peroxide mix:** about one litre of 3% hydrogen peroxide, a quarter cup of baking soda and a teaspoon of dish soap. Work it into the coat (keep it out of the eyes), let it sit five minutes, rinse well. Mix it fresh and never store it in a closed bottle, because it can build up pressure.
3. **Skip the tomato juice.** It just covers the smell for a bit.
4. **Air out the house.** Open windows, run fans.
5. **Call your vet** if the eyes stay red or your dog is pawing at them, or if there was any chance of a bite. Make sure the rabies shots are up to date. Skunks are one of the main rabies carriers in Ontario.

Then call us. If the skunk sprayed from under your deck, that's where it lives, and it'll happen again.

## Is this an emergency?

A skunk passing through your yard at night isn't an emergency. That's just Toronto. But some situations are, and we'll come out as soon as we can:

- **A skunk out in the daytime that's wobbly, circling, or acting fearless or aggressive.** That can be rabies or distemper. Keep kids and pets in and don't go near it.
- **A skunk stuck somewhere,** like a window well, a basement stairwell, a pool or an open trench on a building site.
- **Any bite or scratch** to a person or a pet. For a person, see a doctor and call Toronto Public Health right away. Rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms show up, but treatment after exposure works when it's started in time.
- **Repeat spraying** where kids or pets are getting hit.

Skunks carry other things as well: leptospirosis in their urine, distemper and parvovirus that can pass to dogs, plus ticks, fleas and mites. Most of the time none of that becomes a problem, but it's a good reason not to have a den right where your kids play.

**4. The one-way door.** We fit a one-way door over the main entrance. The skunk pushes out at night to feed and can't get back in. Nobody has to handle the animal, which also means nobody's getting sprayed.

**5. Waiting a few nights.** Usually two to four nights are enough. We check the door and the soil around it to be sure the den is empty.

**6. Sealing it for good.** Once the skunk's out, we dig a trench around the deck, shed or porch and install heavy galvanized mesh in an L shape: down about 30 to 45 cm, then bent outward along the bottom. ### If there are kits under there

If there are kits under there, a one-way door alone can separate mom from babies who can't walk out on their own yet. So we change the plan. Sometimes we wait a few weeks until the kits are following her out at night, then exclude the whole family together. The mother comes back, finds them and carries them off to another den. Either way, no babies get sealed in.

## Decks, sheds and porches: what's different about each

**Decks.** The most common den spot in Toronto by a mile. Low decks with no skirting are the worst, especially the older ones in tight back yards with a laneway behind. We trench and mesh the full perimeter, put the door at the main tunnel, and come back to confirm.

**Sheds.** Sheds on patio stones are basically skunk condos. Rear lot lines backing onto a ravine or rail line get a lot of traffic. Drainage matters here too, because damp soil under a shed is easier to dig and rots the bottom framing. Access can be tight against a fence, so sometimes we have to get creative.

**Front porches.** Older Toronto homes often have concrete porches where the soil underneath has washed away, leaving a hollow. Skunks love it, and the smell goes straight into the basement and front room. We dig carefully so we don't undermine the slab any more, install the L-shaped barrier, and seal the edges with steel flashing and patching. No need to break up the concrete.

**Semis and row houses.** Shared decks, shared porches and mutual driveways mean the den might be half on your neighbour's side. We'll talk to them with you and plan the work so the skunk doesn't just shift next door.

**Construction sites.** Skunks fall into open excavations overnight and can't climb out. If you're a builder or a homeowner mid-reno, we can get the animal out and suggest ramps or covers to stop it happening again.

## What the law says about skunks on your property

Ontario lets you deal with a wild animal that's damaging your property, but with rules. If you live-trap a skunk, you have to release it within one kilometre of where you caught it, and within 24 hours. Hauling it off to a farm field an hour away isn't allowed. Body-gripping traps and poisons are off the table. The province explains the rules on its page about [wildlife damaging private property](https://www.ontario.ca/page/harass-capture-or-kill-wild-animal-damaging-private-property).

The City won't come and remove a healthy skunk from under your deck. That's left to the property owner. The [City's wildlife pages](https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/animals-pets/wildlife-in-the-city/) cover when to call 311 about a sick, injured or dead animal.

Honestly, those relocation rules are the reason trapping makes so little sense. You'd catch the skunk, risk getting sprayed, then let it go a few blocks away, and it'd be back under the same open deck within days. Exclusion skips all of that.

## Why the DIY tricks usually go sideways

I get it. You want it gone tonight. Here's what I see go wrong:

- **Mothballs and ammonia.** Skunks put up with a lot of smell. Mothballs aren't made for outdoor use, and they end up in the soil near your vegetables and pets.
- **Lights and radios.** They might bother a skunk for a night or two. Then it gets used to them.
- **Filling the hole.** In spring, you might bury babies alive. Any time of year, the skunk just digs a new hole, sometimes in a worse spot.
- **Box traps from the hardware store.** You catch the neighbour's cat, or you catch the skunk and then have to carry a trapped, nervous skunk somewhere. Not fun.
- **Poking around with a stick.** A cornered skunk sprays. They usually stamp their front feet and raise the tail first, so if you see that, back away slowly.

## The smell that won't quit

Sometimes the skunk's long gone and the basement still stinks. That's normal, and it's fixable.

Skunk spray is an oily mix of sulfur compounds, and some of them actually get stronger when they get damp. That's why the smell comes roaring back on a humid day or after a rainstorm, even weeks later. Older block foundations and unfinished basements soak it up like a sponge.

We find where it's coming from first. Usually it's the soil right under the den, sometimes insulation or the foundation wall beside it. Then we treat it with oxidizers and enzyme cleaners that break the compounds down instead of covering them up. If something is too saturated, like a piece of fibreglass batt or a cardboard box in storage, it's better to replace it. A dehumidifier in the basement helps a lot during treatment.

## Keeping skunks away for good

Once the den is sealed, the rest is about making your yard less interesting:

- **Lock down garbage and compost** with tight lids, and put bins out the morning of pickup instead of the night before
- **Bring pet food in** before dark, and clean up under bird feeders
- **Pick up fallen fruit** from the lawn
- **Deal with lawn grubs.** No grubs, less digging
- **Check your skirting** every spring for new gaps or soft spots where a skunk has tried to dig
- **Ask us before you build.** If you're putting in a new deck or shed, it's a lot cheaper to add the mesh barrier while it's going in

## Things people ask me in the back yard

**How do I know if there are babies under my deck?**
Between late April and June, listen at dusk for high-pitched squeaks or chatter under the deck. If you see one adult going in and out of the same hole over and over, there are probably kits. Don't block anything until we've had a look.

**Will the skunk spray while you're removing it?**
Not often. The one-way door lets it walk out on its own schedule, so nobody corners it. Most of our jobs don't involve any spraying at all.

**Can I just wait for it to leave?**
It might move on in the fall, but another skunk will probably move into the same spot. If the opening's there, the next one will find it.
