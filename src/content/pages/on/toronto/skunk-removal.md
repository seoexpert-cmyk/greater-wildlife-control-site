## Understanding Skunks in Toronto Neighbourhoods

The striped skunk (*Mephitis mephitis*) is the species Toronto homeowners encounter most frequently. Adults weigh between 2.25 and 3.6 kg, with body lengths up to roughly 45 cm excluding the tail. Their glossy black coat features a distinctive central white stripe or twin stripes running from head to tail. Skunks are nocturnal animals with excellent hearing and a strong sense of smell, but their vision is poor beyond about three metres-which is partly why they startle easily and rely on their scent glands for defence.

Toronto's dense urban fabric is practically designed to attract skunks. Older neighbourhoods like East York, Leslieville, and Bloor West Village feature aging decks without skirting, sheds resting on patio stones, cracked foundations, and small lots packed tightly together. These structural gaps provide the dark, sheltered voids skunks need for denning. Skunks are also drawn by readily available food sources-lawn grubs, compost bins, pet food left outdoors, and fallen fruit. Their powerful forelimbs and long claws make them excellent diggers; skunks can dig 1–3 inch deep holes in lawns searching for grubs, turning a manicured yard into a pockmarked mess overnight. Skunks often create dens under decks, sheds, and porches, and the combination of accessible shelter and abundant food in Toronto's residential areas makes wildlife problems with skunks a recurring concern across the city.

### Seasonal Skunk Behaviour and Nesting Habits

Skunk activity follows a predictable seasonal cycle that directly affects how and when removal should occur. Breeding in southern Ontario happens in late winter, typically February through March. After a gestation period of approximately 60–77 days, skunks can produce litters of 5–6 young in spring, usually in April or May. These baby skunks are born blind and helpless-they open their eyes around three weeks and begin following their mother out of the den at roughly six to eight weeks.

A few terms help clarify what professionals are looking for. A "den site" is any location a skunk uses for shelter. A "natal den" is specifically where the mother gives birth and raises her kits-this is the most sensitive type to disturb. An "overwintering site" is where skunks enter torpor during Toronto's cold months, often deep underground. Homeowners should check for presence of young animals before sealing any den entrances, because disturbing a natal den while babies are immobile risks orphaning them, which is both inhumane and illegal under Ontario's animal welfare standards.

Spring and early summer (April through June) represent peak call season for skunk removal Toronto homeowners request. This is when mothers are denning with dependent kits under sheds and decks. In late summer and fall, skunks forage aggressively to build fat reserves for winter, increasing nocturnal yard activity and the likelihood of encounters with dogs, cats, and humans.

### Typical Skunk Habitat Around Toronto Homes

The most common structural vulnerabilities on Toronto properties that invite skunks include:

* **Open gaps under front concrete porches** where soil has eroded beneath settling slabs

* **Unskirted decks** with no perimeter barrier between the deck frame and ground

* **Floating sheds** resting on patio stones or gravel with easy access underneath

* **Deteriorated garage slabs** with cracked edges or undermined footings

* **Abandoned window wells** without covers or grates

* **Utility line trenches** and improperly backfilled additions that create soil depressions

Skunks exploit any small void, soil depression, or gap along a foundation line to create a den. They don't need large openings-a hole the size of a grapefruit is sufficient access. On a typical Toronto lot, the space between a shed base and the surrounding area of soil, or between a porch footing and the sidewalk, is often more than enough. These features make professional inspection and exclusion critical for lasting results, because sealing one obvious hole while missing a secondary tunnel simply redirects the skunk to another entry point on your property.

### Health and Structural Risks Linked to Skunk Occupancy

Skunks are primary carriers of rabies in Canada, and while rabies incidence in Toronto is low, the rabies virus is usually fatal if untreated after exposure. Beyond rabies, skunks can transmit [leptospirosis](https://www.ontario.ca/page/leptospirosis) and canine distemper through urine and direct contact. Their feces may contain parasitic eggs, and skunks carry parasites like ticks, mites, and fleas that readily transfer to pets and humans. Skunks can also transmit canine parvovirus to pets, making any den near areas where dogs and cats roam a legitimate health concern.

Skunk spray-the oily secretion from their scent glands-contains volatile sulfur compounds (thiols and thioacetates) that are injurious to eyes and respiratory passages. Skunk spray can linger for days on surfaces, and if a skunk sprays near or under your home, the odour can permeate basements and living areas for weeks without proper treatment.

Structurally, skunks often burrow under sheds and decks, causing damage that compounds over time. Chronic burrowing creates soil voids beneath concrete pads, porch footings, and shed bases. Repeated freeze-thaw cycles in Toronto's climate worsen settlement, leading to cracked slabs, sagging steps, and trip hazards. Soil erosion under decks can cause structural timber rot and even allow water ingress into foundations.

These behavioural, seasonal, and structural factors are exactly why a structured, humane removal plan is needed-not a quick fix with mothballs or ammonia.

### Initial Inspection and Site Assessment

Every removal begins with a thorough site assessment. A Greater Wildlife Control technician inspects the full exterior of your property: the foundation line, deck perimeter, shed bases, front and rear steps, air conditioner pads, window wells, and any visible burrow entrances. Technicians should physically inspect the den for baby skunks before sealing any openings-this is a non-negotiable step that prevents orphaning.

The inspection uses specific technical vocabulary that homeowners should understand. "Den confirmation" means verifying that a burrow is actively occupied-not every hole has a skunk in it. "Active vs. inactive burrows" are distinguished by fresh soil disturbance, tracks, hair, and odour. An "entry tunnel" is the primary access point, while a "secondary escape tunnel" is a backup exit skunks often dig as insurance. Soil disturbance patterns, moisture levels, and erosion are all assessed. The technician will also identify the species-distinguishing skunk sign from raccoon or groundhog activity-and estimate whether juveniles may be present based on the season and physical signs at the site.

### Humane Eviction Using One-Way Exclusion Doors

A "one-way exclusion door" is a device installed at a verified den exit that allows skunks to push out and leave but prevents them from re-entering. One-way exclusion doors prevent skunks from re-entering properties without trapping them, making this the preferred humane method for skunk eviction. Best practices for professional skunk removal include humane eviction methods like one-way doors, and removal methods should focus on humane exclusion rather than lethal traps or relocation.

Greater Wildlife Control installs these doors at confirmed entry points under decks, sheds, or concrete porches only after verifying that no skunks are trapped in inaccessible voids. In baby season (late April through June), the approach changes: technicians may gently remove kits by hand, re-nest them near the den entrance in a heated box, or temporarily house them so the mother can relocate them once the one-way door is operational. Live trapping is a last resort for skunk removal, and physical snaring is a humane method used only when exclusion alone cannot resolve the situation. Pets should be kept indoors during skunk removal operations to prevent stress and conflict.

### Structural Exclusion and Long-Term Prevention

Once skunks are confirmed out, the critical step is ensuring they-and other skunks-cannot re enter. This is "wildlife-proofing" or "structural exclusion," and it is what separates professional work from simple trapping.

Greater Wildlife Control installs galvanized steel mesh (16–18 gauge hardware cloth) around deck perimeters, shed bases, and porch edges. Buried L-shaped footings extend 30–45 cm underground and outward, preventing skunks from digging beneath the barrier. Deck skirting is fastened to joists or foundation walls, and all edges are sealed against future intrusion. To permanently prevent re-entry, technicians must seal entry points with durable materials-not chicken wire or wood, which skunks can claw through.

This step addresses the root cause of the skunk issue: accessible crawlspaces. Without it, you are simply removing skunks today and inviting them-or squirrels, raccoons, rabbits, or other wildlife-back tomorrow.

### Biohazard Cleanup and Odour Management

Contaminated soil, droppings, and urine-soaked substrate under dens can harbour pathogens and produce strong odours long after skunks leave. Greater Wildlife Control performs selective excavation of heavily soiled soil, applies enzyme-based disinfectants that are safe for pets and gardens, and uses oxidizing agents to neutralize the sulfur compounds responsible for lingering skunk spray odour. Technicians should advise on eliminating attractants like garbage and compost to prevent skunks from returning to the surrounding area.

In summary, the professional process follows a clear sequence: **inspect → evict → exclude → sanitize**. Each phase builds on the last, and skipping any step compromises the outcome.

### How Do I Know If There Are Baby Skunks Under My Deck?

Between late April and June, listen for high-pitched chattering or squeaking sounds coming from under the structure. Look for small digging marks near the main burrow entrance-kits practice digging as they develop. If you see a female skunk entering and exiting the same hole repeatedly during this period, babies are very likely present. Identifying kits changes the entire eviction strategy, so a professional assessment is essential before any action is taken.

### Will Skunks Spray If You Remove Them?

Skunks spray as a last resort when they feel cornered, suddenly startled, or physically threatened. Poorly handled trapping and aggressive DIY removal are the most common triggers. Professional, low-stress methods-including one-way doors that let skunks leave on their own schedule, calm handling techniques, and strategic positioning-minimize the risk of spray events significantly.
